01Why this memo exists

The comparison was not planned. It became possible when a co-author of the analysis we re-examine handed us the data.

On 31 August 2026 Maximiliano Isi replied to your request for the posterior samples behind the 2021 GW150914 area-law measurement — Ref. [35] of our Supplementary. He does not have that dataset readily available, and pointed instead to the public release for the more recent, higher-SNR result on GW250114: zenodo.org/records/16877102. His stated purpose was that it "should let you extract the horizon areas directly."

That release is the analysis our manuscript re-examines. It contains the collaboration's own posteriors for the same event, the same detectors and the same segment of data. It therefore permits something the manuscript does not currently claim: a direct, like-for-like test of our pipeline against theirs.

This memo reports that test. Every number below is produced by one script, verify_release_crosscheck.py, which reads the release and our own figure data and prints the inputs it used at each step. Nothing in it writes to the manuscript.

04Frame and units, derived rather than assumed

Two conventions the release does not document. Both are fixed from the release's own contents, so neither is a free parameter chosen to improve the agreement.

The frame

The released ringdown products are detector-frame. The conversion factor is not fitted: the release publishes both total_mass and total_mass_source in the same Bilby posterior, and their median ratio is 1.0856 — identical, to three decimal places, to 1 + z from the release's own redshift column, z = 0.0856. That single factor is applied throughout, to the first power for mass and the second for area.

The same posterior gives a median total_mass_source of 65.83 M☉ — the pre-merger mass budget our manuscript quotes, to the digit. This is the first check, and it passes before any conversion is involved.

The unit of the area column

The release does not state the unit of Area_f. Rebuilding the Kerr area from the release's own Mf and χf samples gives a ratio of exactly 1.00000 to the published column, so Area_f is in M☉2. The same test on our own column returns 0.99787, confirming ours is in units of 105 M☉2. The residual 0.2% is the expected difference between the median of a posterior and the value computed from median parameters, not a unit discrepancy.

06Two further channels

One confirms a specific manuscript number. The other confirms the mechanism.

The frequency at the strain peak

Sec. IV.E of the manuscript quotes the constrained template returning 223.2 (219.3, 226.8) Hz at the first start time. The release's own scan gives 224.11 [220.49, 227.63] Hz at t0 = 0, from 4,000 samples — a median difference of 0.41%, with near-identical interval widths.

This is also the most consequential structural fact in the release. Their frequency scan runs from 0 to 30 M. Only the inversion to mass, spin and area begins at 6 M. The raw material for the early window is public; the physical interpretation of it is not.

Overtone content against time

Recomputing the release's own detection statistic — zero_z_score_direct_integration, credited in their script to W. M. Farr — on their own A221 posteriors gives a mean significance of 4.73 σ over t0 = 0–2 M, falling to 0.38 σ over 16–20 M. Overtone content is present early and gone late. That is the trend Sec. IV.G relies on, here from the collaboration's own pyRing amplitudes rather than from the rational-filter analysis we currently cite.

07The horizon areas Isi pointed us to

The specific thing his message offered — and the one result here that is not yet safe to use.

Our Supplementary computes a pre-merger horizon area of Apre = 108,517 (+3,705 / −4,951) M☉2 from the component mass and spin posteriors. The release publishes area_law_inspiral_data.hdf5, holding the inspiral horizon area at 16 different cut-off times, with medians spanning 121,570–129,774 as released, or 103,158–110,120 M☉2 once the same (1+z)2 is applied. Our value falls inside that range.

Must be confirmed before use

The frame of area_law_inspiral_data.hdf5 is not documented anywhere in the release. It is inferred here from the same redshift factor that reconciles the mass and area channels, which is strong evidence but not a statement by the authors. The spread across their 16 cut-offs is also real physics — different inspiral cut-offs give different areas — so "inside the range" is a weaker claim than the point-by-point agreement in section 05.

Recommendation: ask Isi directly, in the reply to his message. It is a one-line question, it is exactly the kind of thing he offered to help with, and the answer converts a caveat into a result.

08What is theirs and what is ours

Stated plainly, because a referee holding this release will ask.

Where the release is stronger

  • Reproducibility, by a wide margin. Per-run MD5 hashes, recorded engine paths, pinned dependencies, one script per figure, a declared single source of truth. Our harness verifies the manuscript; theirs verifies the analysis. A stranger can audit their result end to end and cannot yet audit ours.
  • Sample counts. 120,000-sample runs where precision matters, against our typical 4,000.
  • Detector-level work. PSD estimation, calibration, waveform systematics across four approximants — all of which we inherit rather than reproduce.
  • Scope of the physics. A test of the Hawking area theorem is a larger result than ours.

There is also a quiet convergence worth stating in the paper. Their operating choices — 10.5 M for the single-mode analysis and 6 M for the two-mode — are almost exactly the boundaries we recommend from the data (11 M and 5–6 M). Their practice already embodies our result. What did not exist was the measurement behind it.

09Reproducing every number here

One script, one command, no hidden state.

cd .../PRD_Submission_Kerr_Trajectories/code/figures_v2
python verify_release_crosscheck.py

It reads the release from Downloads/GW250114_data_release and our trajectory from out/fig02_three_channels.csv, prints each input it uses, and writes every value to release_crosscheck.json. It derives the frame and the area unit rather than assuming them, and it writes nothing to the manuscript.

10What follows from this

Three items, in the order they should be done.

  • Resolve the internal inconsistency around Ref. [5]. Sec. I records that the overtone evidence of [5] was "subsequently disputed by Cotesta et al. [11], who attributed much of it to noise." Sec. IV.B then calls the same claim "well established," citing [5]. The paper may now reach the author of [5]. This is a wording fix; it changes no number.
  • State the demarcation and the external validation in Sec. IV.G. The manuscript currently cites [9] only for the inspiral–merger–ringdown consistency values. It does not say that our trajectory reproduces the collaboration's released posteriors, and it does not say where their released inversion begins. Both belong in the text, with the table of section 05 in the Supplementary.
  • Ask about the frame of the inspiral area file. One line, in reply to a message that offered exactly this help.
