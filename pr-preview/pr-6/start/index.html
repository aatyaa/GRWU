Start here

Five short articles for a first-year university student. Each joins a piece of physics, a piece of statistics and a piece of signal analysis, with real data where it can, so that by the end you know what a research analysis of a black hole actually does, and why.

You need school mathematics: sines, exponentials, logarithms, and what a derivative means. Anything beyond that sits in a mathematical layer you can open when you want it.

The symbols

Every article uses these symbols the same way.

ρ\rhoρcorrelation coefficient
A number from −1 to 1 for how closely two quantities move together. It describes a pattern; on its own it is not a cause or a law.Unit: dimensionless.
90% CI90\%\ \mathrm{CI}90% CIcredible interval
A range that holds a stated share of the posterior, often 90%. It describes belief given the model, prior and data, not a guarantee.
τ\tauτdamping time
The time an oscillation takes for its amplitude to fall by a factor e ≈ 2.72.Unit: s.
χ\chiχdimensionless spin
How fast a black hole rotates, χ = cJ/(GM²) for angular momentum J. Zero for a black hole that does not rotate; always below 1.Unit: dimensionless.
ZZZevidence
The probability of the data under a whole model, averaged over its prior. Comparing two models uses the difference ln Z₁ − ln Z₀, the log Bayes factor.
ffffrequency
How many times per second something oscillates.Unit: Hz.
AAAhorizon area
The area of a black hole's event horizon, A = 8π (GM/c²)² (1 + √(1 − χ²)). Hawking's area theorem says the total can only grow.Unit: m², or (G M☉/c²)².
p(d∣θ)p(d \mid \theta)p(d∣θ)likelihood
How probable the data d would be if the parameters were θ. Read as a function of θ with d fixed.
MMMmass
Here, the mass of a black hole, usually in solar masses. Its ring's frequency scales as 1/M: a heavier black hole rings lower.Unit: M☉.
p(θ∣d)p(\theta \mid d)p(θ∣d)posterior
What is believed about the parameters θ after the data d: prior times likelihood, normalised. In practice, a large set of samples.
Sn(f)S_n(f)Sn​(f)power spectral density
How much noise power the detector has at each frequency. Its square root, the amplitude spectral density, is the curve usually plotted.Unit: 1/Hz (for strain).
p(θ)p(\theta)p(θ)prior
What was believed about the parameters θ before looking at these data.
QQQquality factor
How long a ring lasts, counted in oscillations: Q = π f τ. A large Q rings for many cycles.Unit: dimensionless.
(ℓ,m,n)(\ell, m, n)(ℓ,m,n)quasinormal mode
One tone of a ringing black hole: a frequency and a damping time, both fixed by the mass and spin. Labelled (ℓ, m, n); n = 0 is the fundamental and n ≥ 1 are overtones, which fade faster.
zzzredshift
How much the expansion of the universe has stretched a signal on its way. Times and masses read in the detector are larger than at the source by a factor 1 + z.Unit: dimensionless.
fsf_sfs​sampling rate
How many samples of a signal are recorded per second. Frequencies above half the sampling rate, the Nyquist frequency, cannot be told apart from lower ones (aliasing).Unit: Hz.
SNR\mathrm{SNR}SNRsignal-to-noise ratio
How far a signal stands above the noise, measured in units of the noise's typical size after filtering with the expected shape.Unit: dimensionless.
M⊙M_\odotM⊙​solar mass
The mass of the Sun, about 1.99 × 10³⁰ kg. In the units of general relativity it is also a length, G M☉/c² ≈ 1.48 km, and a time, G M☉/c³ ≈ 4.93 μs.Unit: kg.
σ\sigmaσstandard deviation
The typical spread of a quantity around its mean. “nσ” quotes a result's distance from a reference in these units; it assumes independent, bell-shaped errors.Unit: the quantity's own.
hhhstrain
The fractional change in length a passing gravitational wave makes, ΔL/L. The loudest signals seen so far reach about 10⁻²¹.Unit: dimensionless.
